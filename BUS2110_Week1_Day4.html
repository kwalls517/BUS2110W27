Hardware

Hardware comes in five types

Hardware is the physical part of a system. It powers every piece of software a business uses, from a point of sale to a cloud platform.

Input

Enters data. Keyboards, scanners, and sensors.

Processing

Performs calculations and operations. CPUs and graphics processors (GPUs).

Storage

Saves and retrieves data. SSDs and cloud storage.

Output

Presents data to users. Monitors and printers.

Networking

Enables communication. Routers and switches.
Why does a business professional need to know? To choose hardware that controls cost, to decide when to upgrade or replace, and to make sure it works with the business's software.

Four parts do the work inside a computer

Every computer, from a tablet to a server, is built around the same parts.

CPU

The central processing unit. It executes the instructions we give the computer.

Primary memory

Electronic memory the CPU addresses directly. It holds what the computer is working on right now.

System board

The physical circuit board and electrical foundation that connects everything.

Hardware interface

The meeting point between the computer and its peripheral devices, such as the ports for Ethernet, USB, and HDMI.

Software can be built for you or bought ready-made

Custom software

Proprietary applications developed by in-house staff and owned entirely by the organization. Example: Uber's app.

Strengths: competitive advantage, a fit for unique requirements, stronger ROI over time, lower total cost of ownership.

Trade-off: costs more time and money.

Ready-made software (COTS)

Commercial off-the-shelf applications developed by a third party and purchased for use. Example: Canva for social media posts.

Strengths: fast to deploy, plug and play, scalable, 24/7 support, upgrades included.

Trade-off: may not fit your needs perfectly and gives no competitive advantage.

Connect it to Day 3: custom software can create an advantage rivals cannot easily copy. Ready-made software is available to every competitor too.

Develop

Two common ways to run a project

Waterfall

Sequential phases: planning, analysis, design, development, testing, implementation, maintenance. Best for well-defined projects with clear requirements.

Strengths: simple, and each phase has defined deliverables.

Weaknesses: time-consuming, poor for short projects, and cannot handle changing requirements.

Agile

An iterative process with continuous collaboration, built in short cycles called sprints of two to four weeks. Best for dynamic projects where requirements may change.

Strengths: flexible, features are easy to add, and users review after every sprint.

Weaknesses: little documentation, needs skilled people, and risks scope creep and budget creep.

There is no perfect method. You have to examine your needs. Other methods exist, such as the V-shaped, prototype, spiral, and iterative incremental models.

Excel: Chapter 2

Functions do the arithmetic for you

A function is a built-in formula. It starts with =, then the function name, then a range in parentheses. The range C2:C8 means every cell from C2 to C8. Here are five for The Copper Table's weekly sales in C2:C8.

SUM

=SUM(C2:C8)
Adds the numbers. Total sales for the week.

AVERAGE

=AVERAGE(C2:C8)
The mean. Typical sales per night.

MIN

=MIN(C2:C8)
The smallest value. The slowest night.

MAX

=MAX(C2:C8)
The largest value. The busiest night.

COUNT

=COUNT(C2:C8)
How many cells hold numbers. Nights with sales.
Shortcut: AutoSum on the Home tab inserts SUM for you. Always check that the range it highlights is the one you meant.

AI

AI can be a tutor or a shortcut

The same tool can teach you or let you skip the learning. The difference is who does the thinking. Four study moves keep you in charge:

Explain it

Ask for an explanation at your level, with an example, and then a question to check your understanding.

Quiz me

Ask it to question you one at a time and wait for your answer before it replies.

Challenge me

Give it your own explanation and ask what is missing or wrong, without letting it rewrite the answer.

Give me an analogy

Ask for a comparison to something familiar, like a restaurant kitchen, then ask where the analogy breaks down.
Remember: AI can be wrong. When it teaches you something, check it against the class slides or your textbook. On timed Excel work and the certification exam you work without AI, so the learning has to be yours.
