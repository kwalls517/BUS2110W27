Quick check

Three questions before we move on

1. Which components make up information technology (IT)?

IT is only the technology. Procedures and people are what make it an information system.

2. A company buys Tableau licenses for everyone, but nobody is trained and there's no rule for who builds reports. Which components are missing?

The technology is there. Without trained people and clear procedures, it won't produce useful information.

3. What is the main purpose of an information system?

Data is raw facts. An information system turns it into meaningful information that improves decisions and processes.

Analytics

The Copper Table has plenty of data. Does it have information?

Data is raw facts. Information is data organized so it can support a decision. Tap each statement to label it.

Table 12 ordered two Copper Burgers at 7:05 p.m.

A single raw fact. On its own, it doesn't point to a decision.

The Copper Burger was the best-selling dish of the night.

Many orders summarized into one finding. It could shape how much to prep tomorrow.

The kitchen printer printed 143 tickets.

A count with no comparison or context. Is 143 a lot? We can't tell yet.

Tables seated after 8 p.m. waited twice as long for food as tables seated before 7 p.m.

A comparison that reveals a problem. It could support a staffing decision.

A guest's reservation was for 4 people at 8:15 p.m.

One record in the reservation system. Useful to store, but not a finding.

Decide

Before adopting any system, ask what it does for the strategy

Costs or revenue

Would it reduce costs or increase revenue?

Satisfaction

Would it improve customer or employee satisfaction?

Efficiency or effectiveness

Would it help us do things faster, or do the right things better?

Competitive advantage

Would it set us apart from competitors?
Watch for the bandwagon effect. "Everyone else is using it" is not a reason. If a system doesn't support at least one of these goals, it's a cost without a payoff.
Decide

Should The Copper Table buy self-order kiosks?

After opening night, a vendor pitches touchscreen kiosks. The owner says, "Every new restaurant downtown has them." Which argument best justifies the purchase?

That answer ties the system to efficiency and service quality with a concrete reason. "Everyone has them" is the bandwagon effect, and "it uses AI" or "it's on sale" says nothing about the strategy.

Discuss: what data would you want before signing the contract?
