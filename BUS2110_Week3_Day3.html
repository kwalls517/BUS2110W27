1. You enter a markup rate in F1 and write =B2*(1+F1) in C2, then fill down. The results go wrong. What fixes it?

An absolute reference ($F$1) stays fixed when you copy the formula down.

2. Which function shows one result when a condition is true and another when it is false?

Tableau

Tableau builds charts by dragging fields

Excel makes you choose a chart type first. Tableau lets you drop fields onto shelves and builds the chart for you. Every column in your data becomes a field, and every field is either a dimension or a measure.

Dimension (blue)

A category used to split the data. Examples: Channel, Month, Item.

Dimensions give you the groups.

Measure (green)

A number you can add up or average. Examples: Revenue, Profit, Qty.

Measures give you the amounts.

Columns and Rows shelves decide where fields go. A dimension on one shelf and a measure on the other makes a bar chart. Color splits each bar by another dimension.

Recommendation

A recommendation has four parts

1. Finding

What the data shows, with a number.

2. So what

Why it matters to the owner.

3. Action

What to do, specific enough to carry out.

4. Risk

What could go wrong and how you would check.
Example of a weak one: "Delivery is bad." Better: "Delivery earns $13.23 per order versus $33.09 dine-in, so each order that moves to delivery costs about $20. Add a minimum order and track profit per order for a month."

What your team hands in

Write for a manager, not for your professor. A manager should be able to tell quickly what you investigated, what you found, what to do, and why to trust you.

1. Executive summary

The answer in a few sentences.
