Business analytics

Business analytics turns data into decisions

Business analytics is the practice of using data to answer business questions and make decisions. It connects raw data with insights people can act on.

1. Question
2. Data
3. Analysis
4. Decision

Question

What does the business need to know?

Data

What information could answer it?

Analysis

What do the numbers show?

Decision

What will we do, and how will we check it worked?
The analysis is never the goal. A chart no one acts on has not helped the business.
Types of analytics

A doctor's visit shows the three types

You met these three types on Day 5. This week you use them on a real question, so here is the doctor's visit once more. Each step is a different kind of analytics.

Descriptive

What happened?

"I have had a fever for four days." Facts about the past and present.

Predictive

What is likely to happen?

"You probably have the flu." An informed estimate from the pattern.

Prescriptive

What should we do?

"Rest, drink fluids, and take this medicine." A recommended action.

Business metrics

Metrics make performance concrete

A KPI (key performance indicator) is a measurable value that tracks progress toward a business goal. These common metrics appear in almost every role.

Profit margin

How much of each dollar of sales is left after costs.

Market share

Company sales divided by total market sales, times 100.

ROI

Return on investment: (benefit minus cost) divided by cost, times 100.

Churn rate

Customers lost divided by total customers, times 100.
Fixed costs, like rent and salaries, stay the same no matter how much you sell. Variable costs, like ingredients and delivery fees, change with volume. Good pricing and budgeting need both.

Decision-making

Good decisions follow a process

1. Define
2. Gather
3. Analyze
4. Choose
5. Act and check

Define

State the problem and the goal.

Gather

Collect the data that could answer it.

Analyze

Find what the data shows.

Choose

Weigh the options against your priorities.

Act and check

Carry it out and measure the result.
Decision-making

Which step of the process is it?

The owner writes down: "Profit per order is falling. We want to find out why."

Stating the problem and the goal is the Define step.

The manager exports three months of orders from the register system.

An analyst compares profit per order across channels.

Finding what the data shows is Analyze.

The owner picks the option that best fits cost, effort, and risk.

Weighing options against priorities is Choose.

Measuring the result after acting is Act and check.

Excel vs. AI

Excel and AI are good at different things

Excel is good at

Exact, repeatable calculations. Every number can be traced to a formula, and the same steps give the same answer each time.

Weakness: it does only what you tell it, and building something takes skill.

AI is good at

Drafting, explaining, brainstorming, and suggesting approaches quickly, in plain language.

Weakness: it can be wrong while sounding certain, and its answers may not repeat.

The strongest approach uses both: AI helps you think and draft, and Excel proves the numbers.
