Defend

Strong answer or weak answer?

A skeptical owner asks questions. Which answers hold up?

"Why not stop delivery?" — "Delivery still earns $542.50, and some guests only order that way. I would raise what each order earns instead."

It uses a number, respects the trade-off, and proposes something specific.

"Why not stop delivery?" — "Because it is popular."

Popularity is not the measure. It has no evidence.

"How do you know the data is right?" — "I removed two duplicates, fixed one Qty, and matched my totals in Excel and Tableau."

It names what was done and how it was checked.

"How do you know the data is right?" — "The AI said so."

AI is not a source of truth. You must verify.

"What if you are wrong?" — "We would see profit per order stay flat after a month. Then we would try the dine-in offer instead."

It names how you would find out and what you would do.

Presentation and defense

Team presentations

Each team has five minutes to present and about five minutes of questions. Roughly an hour for the whole class.

Present

Question, data and method, findings, recommendation, risks. Lead with the finding.

Defend

Any member can be asked how a number was calculated, why a chart was chosen, how a result was verified, or how AI was used and checked.

Your log

Bring your Consultant Log to the front. You may be asked to return to an earlier entry and explain how your thinking developed.

A new fact

You may be handed one new piece of information and asked how it changes your interpretation or recommendation.
While other teams present: write one question for each team in your log. Good questions ask for evidence, not opinions.

The owner loves delivery growth and dislikes your finding

Your data shows delivery earns far less per order. She asks you to soften it. What do you do?

A good consultant is honest about the evidence and constructive about what to do next. Softening a finding until it disappears helps no one.
