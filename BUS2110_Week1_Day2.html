Define

A business process turns inputs into outputs through a set of activities

Activities

Define

Why should a business care about its processes?

Well-run processes pay off in four ways.

Efficiency

Fewer wasted steps means plates leave the kitchen sooner.

Customer satisfaction

Guests get hot food, the right order, and a short wait.

Reduced costs

Fewer remade dishes and less wasted food.

Better decision-making

A defined process produces data you can measure and compare from night to night.

Cost

Quality

Define

Not every task is a process

Structured

The steps happen in a specific order.

Repeatable

The steps happen again and again.

Goal-oriented

The steps are designed to deliver an outcome.

Tap a label for each statement.

Every night, the kitchen follows the same steps to receive, check, and store food deliveries.

Structured, repeatable, and aimed at a goal: stocked, safe food.

The owner decides to repaint the dining room this summer.

It happens once. It's a project, not something the business repeats.

Each payday, payroll is calculated, approved, and deposited in the same sequence.

A fixed order of steps, repeated every pay period, with a clear goal.

A cook invents a new special on a whim.

No set steps and no repetition. Creativity isn't a process until the business decides how it will happen every time.

The same steps, in order, for every booking, with the goal of a seated guest.

Judge

Good information has five characteristics

Information is only useful if people can trust it for a decision. Check it against five tests.

Accurate

It matches reality. A party of 6 is not logged as 4.

Timely

It arrives when it is still useful. A ticket that prints 20 minutes late is too late.

Relevant

It answers the question being asked. A drinks report does not help with bun orders.

Complete

Nothing needed is missing. A reservation with no phone number cannot be confirmed.

Consistent

Different systems agree. The app and the register show the same price.

Reduces uncertainty

Improves efficiency and outcomes

A formula like =D2*E2 points to cells instead of typing numbers. If a quantity changes, the answer updates itself.

When you AutoFill a formula down a column, Excel shifts the row numbers for you: =D2*E2 becomes =D3*E3.

Number format

Formatting changes how a value looks ($16.00), not what it is (16).

Excel: Chapter 1

Three questions about what Excel just did

1. A table orders 2 burgers at $16 each plus one $5 side. Which formula gives the correct bill?

Excel multiplies before it adds, so 2 times 16 is 32, plus 5 is $37. The parentheses in the second option would charge the side twice ($42). The third multiplies the wrong numbers ($82).

2. You type two in the Qty cell. It lines up on the left, and F2 shows an error. What happened?

Text is left-aligned by default and numbers are right-aligned. Type the digit 2 and the formula works.

3. Why write =D2*E2 instead of =2*16?

Cell references let a spreadsheet respond to changes. Typed numbers freeze the answer.

Verify

How do you know your total is right?

"It looked right" isn't evidence. Today's Consultant Log adds a new question: VERIFY.

Estimate first

Eight rows averaging about $20 each should land somewhere around $160. If Excel says $1,770 or $17, something's off.

Come at it a second way

Total the amounts by a different path, such as by server, and see whether the pieces add up to the same grand total. This is called reconciling.

Spot-check one row

Click a cell and read the formula bar. Does F5 say =D5*E5? Does the answer match what you'd work out by hand?

Does the highest total make Maya the best server? What else would you need to know before you decided anything? (Think about how many tables she had.)
